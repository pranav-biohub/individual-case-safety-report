1. Introduction

An Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) is a structured report used in pharmacovigilance to document suspected adverse reactions associated with medicinal products.

ICSRs contain important information about the patient, suspected medicine, adverse event, clinical outcome, and reporter. These reports help pharmaceutical companies and regulatory authorities monitor medicine safety and identify potential safety signals.

MedDRA (Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities) is an internationally accepted medical terminology used to code adverse events and other medical information in safety databases.

2. Objectives

- Understand the structure and purpose of an ICSR.
- Extract important information from a clinical case narrative.
- Identify suspected and concomitant medicines.
- Map adverse event descriptions to MedDRA Preferred Terms (PT).
- Identify the relevant System Organ Class (SOC).
- Prepare a structured ICSR narrative.

3. Fictional Patient Case Summary

A 42-year-old female patient weighing 60 kg is prescribed amoxicillin 500 mg capsules for a bacterial respiratory infection.

The patient begins treatment on 10 October 2026, taking one capsule three times daily.

On 12 October 2026, approximately one hour after taking the third day's morning dose, the patient develops itchy skin lesions over the body along with swelling of the lips.

The patient is taken to an emergency department and subsequently admitted for overnight inpatient observation and treatment.

The suspected medicine is discontinued. The patient receives appropriate medical treatment, and the symptoms improve. She is discharged after recovery.

No rechallenge is performed.

8. MedDRA Coding and Terminology Mapping

MedDRA provides a standardized terminology for coding medical events reported in pharmacovigilance cases.

Its five hierarchical levels are:

1. System Organ Class (SOC)
2. High Level Group Term (HLGT)
3. High Level Term (HLT)
4. Preferred Term (PT)
5. Lowest Level Term (LLT)

9. ICSR Clinical Narrative

A 42-year-old female patient weighing 60 kg received oral amoxicillin 500 mg three times daily for a bacterial respiratory infection, beginning on 10 October 2026.

On 12 October 2026, approximately one hour after a morning dose, the patient developed generalized itchy skin lesions consistent with urticaria and swelling of the lips consistent with angioedema.

The patient was taken to an emergency department and admitted for overnight inpatient observation and treatment. Amoxicillin was discontinued. The patient showed clinical improvement following treatment and was discharged after recovery.

No rechallenge was performed. The patient's previous allergy history, laboratory findings, and complete concomitant medication history were not available.

The case is classified as serious because it required inpatient hospitalization. Amoxicillin is considered the suspected medicine based on the reported temporal relationship. However, a definitive causal relationship cannot be established from the available fictional information.

11. Causality and Expectedness Assessment

Causality: The temporal relationship between amoxicillin exposure and the onset of urticaria and angioedema supports a suspected association. However, alternative causes and the patient's allergy history have not been fully evaluated.

Expectedness: Unknown. The applicable product information has not been reviewed in this fictional exercise. Expectedness must be assessed against the relevant reference safety information, not assumed from the nature of the reaction.

12. ICSR Data Quality and Missing Information

The following information would be requested during follow-up in an actual pharmacovigilance case:

- Previous allergy and hypersensitivity history.
- Complete concomitant medication history.
- Detailed clinical findings and treatment records.
- Laboratory and diagnostic investigation results, if available.
- Exact admission and discharge dates.
- Relevant product information for expectedness assessment.
- Additional reporter information and confirmation of the case narrative.

Unknown information must remain unknown until verified.

13. Conclusion

The ICSR process is an important component of pharmacovigilance because it ensures that suspected adverse drug reactions are documented in a standardized and structured manner.

This simulated case demonstrates patient information collection, suspected drug identification, adverse event documentation, seriousness classification, MedDRA terminology mapping, and clinical narrative preparation.

The case also highlights the importance of accurate documentation and follow-up information when preparing safety reports.
